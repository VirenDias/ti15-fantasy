Every row is one team per role, with the prefix and suffix that score highest for that combination. Higher is better. Click a column heading to reorder, and scroll for the rest.

How a number is worked out

  • A role scores the best two matches of a series, then the best series of the period. Two maxima, so consistency is worth less than a high ceiling.
  • Expected points come from a team-role's own matches: draw the two or three games of a series, keep the best two, repeat for as many series as they play, and take the best.
  • Both maxima are worked out exactly rather than simulated, so the same banner always gives the same answer.
  • Recent matches count for more, tapering off smoothly rather than at a cut-off.
  • The prefix and suffix are applied to each player in each match before any of that, because which matches they land in is what decides their worth.

The data

  • role-games from patch 7.41 onwards, across 16 teams and three roles.
  • A role-game counts only where a team's role-mates played the same match on the same side, so a stand-in game is left out.
  • Evidence is uneven — from to matches per team and role. A best-of-several figure flatters the thin ones.
  • of Bo3s go to three games, measured from the same matches rather than assumed.

What is assumed

  • The grand final is treated as a Bo3.
  • Series are drawn independently, so a team on a run of form is not modelled.
  • the Clutch is overstated. It fires on 29.6% of matches here against roughly 17% in an all-Bo3 tournament, because a Bo1 match trivially counts as the last possible one. Discount it.
  • the Cruel cannot be priced and is left out — it needs a fountain-death count the replay parser does not yet produce.
  • How far a team goes is not predicted. That is the one input left to you.

The full record, with the measurements behind each decision, is in methodology.md in the repository.
