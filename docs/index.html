How to use this
  1. Set your War Banners to match the game: the stat, quality and trait of each of the nine emblems.
  2. Open the War Banner Optimiser tab and set the three roll operations the game is offering you.
  3. Read the recommendation and the table beneath it. The recommendation names the single best move. The table gives the outcome of every action open to you: each option on each banner it applies to, and rerolling for a new set.

    Note: a roll can leave a banner worse than it was, and the Chance worse column gives the odds of that happening. Expected is still the figure that decides. It is the average change across every outcome, weighted by how likely each one is, so an option can be worth taking even with a high Chance worse, as long as its rare good outcomes are big enough. Click a row in the table to see all the outcomes behind it.

  4. Update your banners after each token you spend, then repeat from step 2.
  5. Open the Roster & Titles Optimiser tab once your banners are settled.

    Note: titles are free to change and banners are not, so there is nothing to gain from doing this earlier.

  6. Choose a row. Each one names a team for every role together with the prefix and suffix to run alongside them.

    Note: the roster and the titles have to be decided together. One prefix and one suffix cover all five players, so what a title is worth depends on which teams you pick, and which teams are best depends on the titles. That is why every row comes with its own pair rather than letting you choose them separately.

  7. Tick Show expected points for every possible number of series if you think a team will play more or fewer series than the median.

    Note: the tables otherwise assume every team plays the median number of series. How far a team actually goes is the one thing this cannot predict.

Expected points are for the median number of series played across the entire period.

This is for anyone who wants to see the numbers underneath. They are the same calculation the optimisers run — the whole period, at the median number of series, with no emblem multipliers. One thing does not carry over: each emblem stat below is measured on its own, so the figures do not add up to what a three-emblem banner scores. Each gets to pick its own best games, while a real banner has to pick one set for all three.

Expected points per emblem stat, by role

Total points each emblem stat is expected to score over the period for a role, averaged across all teams, before any multiplier. Emblem stats this period's banner cannot hold are dimmed.

Expected multiplier per title, by team and role

What each title is expected to add over the period, for a given team and role, as a percentage of the same banner without it. Judged on every emblem stat the role can hold, so it does not depend on which three a banner happens to carry.

Data

  • The data is taken from professional matches played on patch or later, covering all 16 teams at the tournament.
  • The data comes from both OpenDota and the match replays themselves, read using a custom parser built for this.
  • Matches are exponentially weighted, so recent ones count for more.
  • Coverage is uneven, between and matches for a given team and role.
  • Much of the data is Bo1 and Bo2, where nearly every game is trivially the last possible match of its series. That makes the recorded trigger for the Clutch far more common than it will be at the tournament, so it cannot be read from the data at all.
  • The data needed for is not available yet. We are working on acquiring it.

Assumptions

  • How far a team goes is not predicted. A team plays , and the tables assume the median of each — series respectively.
  • The grand final is a Bo5 but is modelled as a Bo3. Only the best two matches of a series count either way, and it is a single series, so the difference is minimal.
  • Reroll odds are not published. Valve states only that Emblem Quality is weighted against the higher tiers, so the odds are assumed to fall off in inverse proportion to the boost — for tiers I to V. Emblem Traits and Stats, and which three roll operations are offered, are taken as equally likely.

Calculation

  • A role scores the average of its players, counting only the stats on their banner. The best two matches of a series count, then the best series of the period — two maxima in sequence, which rewards a high ceiling over consistency.
  • The result is therefore not a mean but the expected value of that maximum. Every pair of games a series can produce, and every best-of-several a period can produce, is enumerated and weighted by its probability. A series runs two or three games in the proportion observed in the data, where of Bo3s go to three. Nothing is simulated.
  • Those games are drawn to match how a Bo3 actually plays out. Two games means one side swept, so both are wins or both are losses; three games means the first two were split, which is what took it to a third, and the decider goes to whoever takes the series. Drawing freely would invent 3-0 series and split two-game ones, neither of which can happen.
  • Prefixes and suffixes are applied to each player in each match, before either maximum is taken, because which matches they fall in determines their value. They can be changed freely, so roll evaluation excludes them.
  • The Clutch is the exception. It pays out in the last possible match of a series, which is a position rather than a property of the match, so it is applied to the third game of a series that runs to three and to nothing at all in a series that ends 2-0. That game is drawn like any other, and deciding games tend to score a little above average, so its worth is slightly understated.
  • Since the team is chosen later, a banner is valued as the mean of what it is worth to each of the 16 teams in that role. Valuing it against only the strongest team was tested and made very little difference to which rolls come out ahead, so the simpler measure is used.
  • Taking an option and rerolling both cost one token and both replace all three options, so the only difference between them is whether the operation is applied. A reroll therefore carries an expected change of zero, and an option is worth taking only if it exceeds zero. Where no operation gains on any banner, a new set of options cannot contain one either.
  • Every outcome of an operation is enumerated and weighted by its probability, and a reroll cannot return the value an emblem already holds. The expected change is the deciding figure: an option with a positive expectation may still be more likely to lower a banner than to raise it, where the gain in its rare favourable outcomes is large enough.
